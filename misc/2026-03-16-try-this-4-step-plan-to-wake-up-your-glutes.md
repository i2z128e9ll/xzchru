# Try This 4-Step Plan to Wake Up Your Glutes

Kinesiologist and fitness trainer Jeremy Ethier of Built with Science wants to help you "awaken" your glutes.

"The problem nowadays is that we tend to sit a lot, and for prolonged periods of time, which is a great way to forget how to use your glutes. Especially if you don't take action to counteract this," says Ethier.

This can often lead to lower back, hip pain, and gluteal amnesia (a.k.a. dead butt syndrome), a condition that occurs when your glutes are unable to fire. Spine expert Dr. Stuart McGill, who created the The McGill Big 3 core stability exercises, coined the term in his research.

There are four indicators that you may be experiencing this condition:

One, you have a hard time feeling your glutes working/contracing. "If you feel it in your back and hamstrings during lower body exercises instead of your glutes, this is a sign the glutes aren't activating how they should be," says Ethier.

Two, you don't feel your glutes when you perform a single-leg glute bridge. "With one leg straightened and the other leg bent on the floor, hold this position with your hips in the air. If you feel your hamstrings, lower back or your quads more so than your glutes, then this is another indication that your glutes aren't doing what they're supposed to be doing during hip extension."

Three, you exhibit an anterior pelvic tilt. "If from the side you experience an anterior pelvic tilt posture (13 degrees), then your glutes are likely failing to pull your posture to its neutral position due to weakness," says Ethier.

Four, you have a hard time filling out your pants. "If you sit for most of the day and you have what I call Flat Butt Syndrome, then chances are your glutes can use some work," says Ethier.

If you have one or more of these indicators, you can try this four-step glute activation plan.

Step 1: ContractionThe first step is to understand what glute contraction feels like. To do this, sit down and place one hand under each butt cheek. Take turns contracting each of your glutes to make the mind-body connection.

From there, a more advanced mode is to get on the floor onto one knee. Contract the glute on your leg that hits the floor to feel your glutes engaging. Do this a few times on each leg.

Step 2: ActivationThis step require two simple exercises from Dr. Stuart McGill that have been found to get your glutes firing again. Dr. McGill walked Ethier through each exercise, and he's giving the full scoop below:

Exercise 1: Glute Bridges (3 sets of 10 reps with 5 second pause at top)This move targets the largest of your glute muscles, the gluteus maximus.

"Lay on your back with your knees bent. Keep your core braced and back flat, and squeeze your butt to get them engaged first. Then lift up while keeping your glues contracted. At the top, keep your glutes squeezed as hard as possible for about 5 seconds before coming back down," says Ethier.

Exercise 2: Clam Shells (3 sets of 10 reps per side)

This will help activate your gluteus medius.

Lay on your side with your knees and hips bent. Use one arm to make a pillow for your head and then with your other hand, place your thumb on the bone in front of your hip and then wrap your other fingers around over top the upper part of your butt (the glute medius). You want to feel this muscle working as you do the movement. While keeping your feet together and core braced, open up your top knee like a clam shell so that the knee of your upper leg rises toward the ceiling. "Avoid rotating your hips as you do so by maintaining that abdominal bracing," says Ethier.

Step 3: Progression with Resistance LoadOnce you feel comfortable with the two moves, you can progress the work you're doing for your glutes with more resistance and load. To do this, there are three exercises you should be doing.

Exercise 1: Lateral Step Up"It challenges the gluteals in hip extension, but also in frontal plane strength," says Dr. McGill.

To do it, stand on a block/step with one leg. Keep your arms out forward for balance, and touch the heel of the other leg to the floor. Then come back up. Once you progress, you can pull your knee up at the top and hold for 1 to 2 seconds.

Exercise 2: Goblet Squat"It targets the glutes and really stimulates all of the neuromuscular compartments in the gluteals," says Dr. McGill.

To do it, hold a kettlebell or dumbbell at your chest. Use your lats to screw down your shoulder sand hold your elbows in. Squat back and pull your hips through. For a progression, squat down 3/4 of the way then shift side to side over each foot.

Exercise 3: Cable Pull-Throughs"It really sets up squats and deadlifts and some of the more 'power' types of exercises," says Dr. McGill.

To do it, grab the cable through your legs. Bend at your hips and pull through back to an upright position.

At the end of this training progression, the idea is to have full integration of hamstrings and gluteals," says Dr. McGill.

Step 4: Prevention"You want to avoid long periods of sitting where you just aren't using your glutes at all," says Ethier. Every 30 minutes you should get up, take a walk or have a stretch.

He suggests doing wakeup exercises during these breaks, maybe 3 to 5 times throughout the day. Try toe raises—point your toes outwards, squeeze the quads, and then rise up on your toes while squeezing your glutes as hard as possible. Do that with 10 reps of 5 second holds.
