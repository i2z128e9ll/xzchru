How to Ask Your Doctor When to Stop Ashwagandha Before Surgery

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

When should you stop taking ashwagandha before surgery? Most experts recommend discontinuing ashwagandha and other herbal supplements 7 to 14 days before your procedure. However, the exact timing depends on your anesthesia type, current medications, and overall health status.

Below, you'll find complete guidance on preparing for your pre-surgery doctor conversation, sample questions to ask, and essential safety tips to ensure nothing important is overlooked.

Explanation

Ashwagandha is a popular herbal supplement often used to reduce stress, boost energy, and support overall well-being. While many people take it daily, combining ashwagandha and surgery risk requires careful planning. Herbal supplements can affect anesthesia, blood pressure, and bleeding. Knowing when and how to stop ashwagandha before your procedure helps you stay safe.

Why Discuss Ashwagandha Before Surgery?

Most surgeons and anesthesiologists need a full picture of everything you take, including:

Prescription medications

Over-the-counter drugs

Vitamins and minerals

Herbal supplements (like ashwagandha)

Herbal ingredients can:

Interact with anesthesia or pain medications

Alter blood pressure or heart rate

Understanding Ashwagandha and Surgery Risk

Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) has active compounds that may:

Lower blood pressure (hypotensive effect)

Act as a mild sedative

Potentially affect thyroid function

Influence immune response

Possible concerns during surgery:

Bleeding risk• Some herbs thin the blood or inhibit platelets. While research on ashwagandha's direct impact on clotting is limited, combining it with other blood-thinners (like aspirin or warfarin) raises concerns.

Blood pressure swings• Anesthesia can lower blood pressure. If ashwagandha also lowers it, you might become hypotensive during surgery.

Sedation and anesthesia interactions• Ashwagandha may have a calming effect. Adding sedatives or anesthetics on top of that can lead to over-sedation.

Hormonal shifts• If you have thyroid or adrenal issues, ashwagandha may alter hormone levels, potentially affecting how you respond to anesthesia and recovery.

When to Stop Ashwagandha Before Surgery

Most experts recommend stopping herbal supplements at least 1–2 weeks before surgery. Here's a general guideline:

Minor procedures (local anesthesia): stop 7 days prior

Major surgery (general anesthesia): stop 10–14 days prior

Always follow your surgeon's or anesthesiologist's exact instructions. They may tweak the timing based on:

Your overall health

Other medications and supplements you take

The type and length of surgery

Note your surgery details

Date and time of procedure

Be ready to answer questions about your medical history

Heart, blood pressure, or thyroid issues

Allergies or previous surgical complications

Sample Questions to Ask

Use these prompts to guide your conversation:

"I take ashwagandha daily for stress. Can you advise when I should stop before my [type] surgery?"

"Are there any special risks if I continue ashwagandha right up to the procedure?"

"Should I watch for changes in my blood pressure or heart rate while stopping ashwagandha?"

"Do you recommend any labs (like thyroid or clotting tests) before surgery because of ashwagandha?"

"Will stopping ashwagandha early affect my recovery or pain management plan?"

What to Expect from Your Doctor

Your surgeon or anesthesiologist should:

Review your entire medication/supplement list

Provide a clear stop-date for ashwagandha

Explain any monitoring needed (blood pressure checks, blood tests)

Offer a tapering schedule if sudden discontinuation may cause withdrawal symptoms (rare)

Outline changes to your pre-op and post-op care plan

Special Considerations

Thyroid patients: ashwagandha may influence your thyroid hormone levels.

Blood-thinners: notify if you're on warfarin, clopidogrel, or similar.

Adrenal issues: stopping adaptogens like ashwagandha can change cortisol levels.

If in doubt, your healthcare team may order blood tests (e.g., thyroid panel, clotting profile) before clearing you for surgery.

Final Reminders

Never stop or change any medication or supplement unless instructed by your surgeon or anesthesiologist.

Be clear and honest about all supplements you take—no detail is too small.

Plan ahead: herbal products often have a drug-elimination window.

Speak to your doctor about any serious or life-threatening concerns. If you notice severe symptoms—such as unusual bleeding, chest pain, or difficulty breathing—seek emergency care right away.

Your safety is the top priority. Open communication with your healthcare providers about ashwagandha and surgery risk ensures the best outcome for your procedure and recovery.
