Numbness in the left pectoral muscle is often caused by nerve irritation from a tight or overworked chest muscle, a condition known as pectoralis minor syndrome. It can also result from intercostal neuralgia, which occurs when an intercostal nerve between the ribs becomes compressed or inflamed.

Other possible causes include poor posture, nerve compression in the neck or shoulder, muscle strain, or, less commonly, cardiovascular issues. Because chest numbness can range from a minor muscular problem to something more serious, identifying the underlying cause is essential for choosing the right treatment.

Explanation

One common cause is pectoralis minor syndrome. This happens when the pectoralis minor muscle, located beneath the pectoral muscles, becomes tight or overworked. It can press on nerves that come from the brachial plexus under the collarbone. When these nerves are squeezed, you might feel numbness in the pectoral area. Another possibility is intercostal neuralgia. The intercostal nerves run between the ribs and help make the chest and pectoral muscles feel normal. If one of these nerves is irritated by injury, strain, or even inflammation, numbness (and sometimes pain) might appear. It's also important to remember that sometimes other nearby conditions, like issues with the chest wall, can cause similar feelings.
