What is fatty15?

Fatty15 (also known as FA15) is a pentadecanoic acid (C15:0) supplement designed by Drs. Stephanie and Eric Venn-Watson. Dr. Eric Venn-Watson worked for over 25 years as a physician in the Navy and Marine Corps, and Dr. Stephanie Venn-Watson is a veterinary epidemiologist and researcher.

While studying dolphin health, they noticed that dolphins who ate fish with a greater fatty acid content lived longer and were generally healthier. They expected that dietary omega-3s would be the defining factor, as older, healthier dolphins ate more fatty fish. However, the difference wasn't in omega-3s but rather in the amount of C15:0 in their diet. Considering we share many biological and cellular mechanisms with dolphins, they began studying if C15:0 could impact human health in the same ways and published their findings in Scientific Reports in 2020.2 Fatty15 was created soon after.

Seraphina Therapeutics, the parent company of fatty15, has exclusive licensing rights from the U.S. Navy for C15:0, protecting the commercialization and usage of C15:0 as a supplement, food or beverage ingredient, or pharmaceutical. The company also has over 40 patents covering the usage, dosing, and formulation of C15:0 to support various health conditions.

Each fatty15 vegan capsule contains 100mg of C15:0 made at a cGMP-compliant, FDA-approved manufacturing facility. The product is derived from plant-based fatty acids, most commonly sourced from nutmeg (no nutmeg is left remaining in the completed product). Fatty15 is extensively tested in-house and by an independent third party for the purity of every batch.

The makers of fatty15 suggest you take one capsule daily, with or without food.

Fatty15 has been given generally recognized as safe (GRAS) status by the FDA and should be safe for most people to take. It also appears to be safe for those who are pregnant or breastfeeding.

How does fatty15 work?

To start off, let’s examine essential fatty acids. We need them to maintain our health, but these fatty acids aren’t synthesized (created) by the body, so we have to consume them through our diet.32 C15:0 (fatty15) is an odd-chain (based on the number of carbon atoms it carries) fatty acid found in whole-fat dairy products, particularly butter. Though we’ve known of its existence since the 1940s, most research around C15:0 has taken place in the last decade, uncovering its potential as an essential fatty acid in human health.

Also known as pentadecanoic acid, C15:0 is a saturated fatty acid, so it does not contain any double bonds that are prone to oxidation.6 Saturated fats have long been considered “bad” when compared to unsaturated fats like essential fatty acids omega-3 and omega-6. But while even-chain saturated fats are linked to a higher risk of heart disease and type 2 diabetes,7 recent discoveries around C15:0 reveal that this odd-chain fatty acid can have just the opposite effect.8

A 2013 study of over 2,800 adults published in the Journal of the American Heart Association revealed the inverse association between plasma C15:0 levels and risk factors for cardiovascular disease.9 In other words, people with a higher circulating C15:0 concentration had a lower risk of cardiovascular disease. In 2014, an additional large cohort study pointed out yet another inverse relationship, this time between C15:0 blood levels and type 2 diabetes.10

In 2020, Dr. Venn-Watson and two other scientists published a study in Scientific Reports investigating C15:0’s potential role in human health after finding it helped dolphins’ metabolic health as they age.2 3 This paper directly suggests that C15:0 plays a role in stemming:

Inflammation

Anemia

High cholesterol

Fibrosis (hardening or scarring of tissues)11

Most notably, the study proposed that C15:0 is an essential fatty acid that might bind to metabolic regulators and repair mitochondrial function. Mitochondrial functioning is an important clue into C15:0’s role in the human body since the mitochondria, as the powerhouses of the cell, control our cellular energy. At the same time, another 2020 study by a separate group of researchers found that C15:0 had an anticancer effect against a particularly aggressive form of breast cancer.12

It’s worth mentioning that the studies on C15:0 thus far have been animal or human-cell-based studies. However, a clinical trial researching C15:0 supplementation in young adults at risk for metabolic syndrome appears to be underway.4

Endocannabinoids and C15:0

In August 2022, Dr. Venn-Watson discovered that one metabolite of C15:0 is an endocannabinoid.13 These chemicals are naturally created in the brain and have the same impact as cannabinoids like CBD and THC. In this study, pentadecanoylcarnitine (PDC), the cannabinoid that comes from C15:0, acted as an anti-inflammatory agent across five different systems: cardiovascular, immune, neoplastic (abnormal tissue growths and tumors), pulmonary, and skin.

Anti-aging drugs and C15:0

A very recent (October 2023) article published in Nutrients examined C15:0 along with three longevity-enhancing prescription drugs: acarbose, metformin, and rapamycin.5 Using human-cell-based assays, researchers were able to compare each compound’s ability to enhance processes associated with longevity. In other words, these tests indicated what impact each substance would have on different disease states.

C15:0 performed well and had the most clinically relevant, dose-dependent activities. Rapamycin and C15:0 notably shared anti-inflammatory, antifibrotic, and anticancer activities. C15:0 also exhibited similarities in behavior to metformin. Researchers concluded that C15:0 could be a safe, non-prescription supplement that supports longevity just as well as other well-known drugs.5

A 2022 study by Dr. Stephanie Venn-Watson (creator of fatty15) and her colleagues compared the clinical effects of omega-3 and C15:0 using a human primary cell disease model.25 Specifically, they tested four different concentrations of eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) and C15:0, as EPA is one of the most thoroughly researched omega-3 fatty acids. Alternative supplements to combat cellular aging

Fatty15 is currently the only C15:0 supplement available. With Seraphina Therapeutics’ numerous patents for C15:0, we don’t anticipate that changing anytime soon. However, if you’re looking for an alternative to fatty acid supplementation to tackle the cellular aging process, there are some other options to consider.

Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD)

Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD) is a coenzyme found in our cells that is critical to energy metabolism. NAD decreases as we age, affecting our body’s ability to repair DNA and regulate our immune system. However, there are supplements available that aim to boost NAD as a way to fight cellular aging.28

NAD supplements are generally safe, although there is a lack of clinical trial data reflecting long-term side effects.29 The cost of these supplements varies wildly, depending on the brand and ingredients included. If NAD boosters have grabbed your interest, check out our complete guide to explore in more detail how these supplements work and what the competitive landscape has to offer.

Nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN)

Nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) is a precursor to NAD and is rapidly absorbed and converted when taken orally. Some prefer NMN over NAD directly since it’s smaller and can pass through cell membranes more easily. However, NMN supplements, like all NAD precursors, can have bioavailability issues, depending on the delivery method. Despite this, research shows that NMN effectively raises NAD levels, allowing you to reap the benefits of potentially improved longevity.30

Like NAD supplements, NMN does not appear to have negative side effects with short-term usage, but more clinical studies are needed to assess long-term safety. As for cost, NMN supplements are generally going to cost more than fatty15, but there is a wide range of price points depending on the product and delivery method of NMN. FAQ about fatty15

How long does it take fatty15 to work?

Fatty15 claims that some customers see benefits within a few weeks of starting the supplement. This can include improved sleep, calmer mood, and more energy. More long-term benefits are said to begin 1-3 months after beginning fatty15 once a day.

What are the benefits of taking fatty15?

Fatty15 contains C15:0, an essential fatty acid. This nutrient works on a cellular level to regulate metabolism, mood, and immunity, among other systems. The makers of fatty15 believe that taking this supplement can slow down or reverse cellular aging, potentially improving longevity and overall health. Does fatty15 replace omega-3s?

Research studies indicate that fatty15 can benefit your overall health in both similar and different ways from omega-3s.25 Fatty15 can replace omega-3s, but there is also no indication that taking both supplements together will be harmful. If you have been prescribed omega-3s, please discuss any potential supplement changes with your doctor.

Does fatty15 raise cholesterol?

Unlike even-chain saturated fats, the odd-chain C15:0 found in fatty15 is not believed to raise cholesterol. In fact, an animal study found that 12 weeks of supplementation with C15:0 actually lowered total cholesterol. Other human cohort studies have shown that higher blood levels of C15:0 correlate with lower cholesterol and triglycerides, as well.2

Is fatty15 better than fish oil?

Both of these essential fatty acid products can offer you health benefits, including anti-inflammatory and heart health perks. Drawbacks of fish oil include its susceptibility to oxidation and the fact that many people find the smell and taste of some fish oil supplements to be off-putting. But fatty15, while promising, is a newer supplement on the market, so there’s a lack of human clinical trial data to solidify its status as the superior product. However, fatty15 can be an alternative for vegans, vegetarians, or those who cannot tolerate fish oil.

What is the source of fatty15?

C15:0 is found in whole-fat dairy products (like butter) and some plants and fish. The C15:0 in fatty15 is synthesized to match that found in nature. Fatty15’s starting fatty acid material is sourced from nutmeg and then converted to C15:0, with no nutmeg remaining in the completed compound.
