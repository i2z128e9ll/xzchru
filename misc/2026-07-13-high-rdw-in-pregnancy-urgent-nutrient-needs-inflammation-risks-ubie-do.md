Explanation

If your RDW blood test is high during pregnancy, it's normal to have questions. Pregnancy brings major changes to your blood, immune system, and nutritional needs. While some lab values shift naturally, a high RDW (Red Cell Distribution Width) can sometimes signal that your body needs extra support—especially when it comes to iron and other key nutrients.

Let's break down what this means, why it happens, and what you can do next.

What Is RDW?

RDW (Red Cell Distribution Width) is part of a standard complete blood count (CBC). It measures how much variation there is in the size of your red blood cells.

Normal RDW: Red blood cells are fairly uniform in size.

High RDW blood test result: There is more variation in red blood cell size than usual.

Red blood cells carry oxygen throughout your body—and during pregnancy, they support both you and your growing baby. When red blood cells vary widely in size, it often points to a problem with how they are being produced.

Why RDW Can Be High in Pregnancy

Pregnancy increases your blood volume by up to 50%. Your body works harder to produce enough red blood cells to keep up with this demand. If something interferes with healthy red blood cell production, RDW may rise.

The most common causes include:

1. Iron Deficiency (Most Common Cause)

Iron deficiency is extremely common in pregnancy. According to major obstetric and hematology guidelines, iron needs nearly double during pregnancy.

Iron deficiency can cause:

Smaller red blood cells (microcytosis)

Increased variation in cell size

Rising RDW before hemoglobin even drops

In fact, an RDW blood test high result can be one of the earliest signs of iron deficiency, even before anemia develops.

2. Folate or Vitamin B12 Deficiency

Folate needs increase significantly during pregnancy. Low folate or B12 can cause:

Larger-than-normal red blood cells

Greater size variation

Elevated RDW

Folate deficiency is especially important to address because it plays a critical role in fetal neural development.

3. Inflammation

Pregnancy itself involves immune changes, but chronic inflammation from:

Autoimmune conditions

Infections

Obesity

Chronic diseases

can affect red blood cell production. Research shows that inflammatory states can increase RDW levels.

4. Mixed Nutrient Deficiencies

It's possible to have:

Low iron and

Low folate or B12

When both small and large red blood cells are present, RDW often increases even more.

Is a High RDW Dangerous During Pregnancy?

A high RDW blood test result alone is not a diagnosis. It is a clue.

What matters most is:

Hemoglobin level

Hematocrit

MCV (mean corpuscular volume)

Ferritin (iron stores)

If your hemoglobin is normal and you feel well, your provider may simply monitor levels.

However, untreated anemia during pregnancy can increase the risk of:

Fatigue and weakness

Preterm delivery

Low birth weight

Postpartum complications

This is why identifying the cause early is important.

You may want to pay attention if you notice:

Unusual fatigue

Shortness of breath

Dizziness

Pale skin

Headaches

Rapid heartbeat

Cravings for ice or non-food items (pica)

Why Iron Needs Increase "For Two"

During pregnancy, your body must:

Produce more red blood cells

Supply oxygen to the placenta

Support fetal blood development

Prepare for blood loss at delivery

Daily iron requirements increase from about 18 mg (non-pregnant) to 27 mg per day during pregnancy.

If intake doesn't keep up, your body pulls from stored iron (ferritin). Once stores are depleted, red blood cell production changes—and RDW may rise.

How Doctors Evaluate a High RDW

If your RDW is elevated, your doctor may order:

Ferritin (iron storage test)

Serum iron and transferrin saturation

Vitamin B12 level

Folate level

Reticulocyte count

C-reactive protein (to assess inflammation)

This helps pinpoint the root cause rather than guessing.

When Treatment Is Needed

Treatment depends on the cause.

If Iron Deficiency Is Confirmed:

Oral iron supplements are usually first-line.

Some women may need intravenous (IV) iron if levels are very low or oral iron isn't tolerated.

If Folate or B12 Is Low:

Supplementation is typically very effective.

Prenatal vitamins often correct mild deficiencies.

Most nutritional causes improve within weeks to months with proper treatment.

Can RDW Go Back to Normal?

Yes.

When the underlying issue is corrected:

Red blood cell production stabilizes

Cell size becomes more uniform

RDW gradually decreases

Keep in mind that it can take several weeks for blood markers to fully normalize.

When to Seek Immediate Medical Care

While a high RDW blood test result alone is not an emergency, you should seek urgent medical care if you experience:

Severe shortness of breath

Chest pain

Fainting

Heavy vaginal bleeding

Signs of preterm labor

These symptoms can indicate serious complications and require immediate attention.

Practical Steps You Can Take Now

If your RDW is high during pregnancy:

✅ Review your full blood count results with your provider

✅ Ask whether ferritin was checked

✅ Take your prenatal vitamin consistently

✅ Eat iron-rich and folate-rich foods daily

✅ Avoid taking iron with calcium (it reduces absorption)

✅ Follow up on repeat labs if recommended

Most importantly, do not self-diagnose or self-treat with high-dose supplements without medical guidance.

The Bottom Line

A high RDW blood test result during pregnancy often reflects increased nutritional demands—especially iron deficiency. In many cases, it is manageable and reversible with proper care.

Pregnancy places extraordinary demands on your body. Monitoring labs like RDW helps ensure both you and your baby are getting the oxygen and nutrients needed for healthy development.
