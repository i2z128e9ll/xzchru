# LeBron James Is Struggling to Cast ‘Space Jam 2’

It's just about time to say 'That's All, Folks!' on LeBron James' first season in Los Angeles—not only will he miss the NBA Finals for the first time since 2009, but his Lakers didn't even come close to a playoff spot. The sluggishness isn't limited to the Staples Center, or even to this realm of reality: it's been reported that LeBron has also been struggling to fill out the roster of his much-awaited Space Jam 2 project, with the sequel's cast looking bare beyond Bugs and Daffy as of now.

As Brian Windhorst mentioned on ESPN's The Jump with Rachel Nichols, LeBron has been trying to get a number of his peers to join him in the follow up to Michael Jordan's 1996 cult/nostalgia classic (which saw Charles Barkley, Patrick Ewing, Larry Johnson, Mugsy Bogues, and Shawn Bradley among those who joined His Airness on screen), but has been struggling to get the guys he wants to sign on the dotted line. “In all honesty, he's been recruiting players to come and try to be in 'Space Jam' with him this summer, and he hasn't been able to close some of those deals,” Windhorst said.

He continued to elaborate on the reasoning why some of the potential stars were hesitant to join the Space Jam 2 cast. "Some of the top guys he wants to come and be with him in the movie. ... ‘You put me in the movie where you're the star, I'm gonna be the one you're dunking on.’ ”

This... however... shows a fundamental misunderstanding of what Space Jam really even is. The original Space Jam didn't see Michael Jordan yamming it home on Charles Barkley or Patrick Ewing—quite the opposite, in fact. He was their friend, and it was mostly a chance for these guys to show off some comedy skills. Barkley, in fact, shined bright in his role, showing a penchant for comedy that would eventually come in handy both in his role as an analyst on Inside The NBA or on his many appearances hosting Saturday Night Live.

A few of LeBron's targets for the movie should be fairly obvious. He's clearly going to want a combination of players he's both friendly with (Chris Paul, Dwyane Wade) and guys who have become indispensable faces of the league (Steph Curry, Kevin Durant, James Harden). Not to mention players who are just such close parallels that it might be harder to not have them in the movie than the other way around—Draymond Green, for one example, is so similar to Barkley both on the court, and with his outsized,braggadocios personality, that it would be a misstep not to include the versatile forward in the movie.

This isn't to say that LeBron is coming up completely empty. There would without doubt be many players who are basically salivating at the mouth for the chance to step into potential NBA lore for years to come. For one, while it seems like Nick Young's playing days might be just about over, the man known as Swaggy P recently made a passionate pitch for King James to include him in the upcoming film.

"You gotta have a Swaggy in Space Jam. If you want to sell tickets, you got to have me," he told Warriors writer Mark Medina. "I can give them swag once they get their powers taken. They can come to me & I can be a sorcerer of Swag. I can hand it out to them & give them hope."
