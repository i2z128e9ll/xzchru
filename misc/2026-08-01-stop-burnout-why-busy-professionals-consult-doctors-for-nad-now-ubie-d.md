What Is NAD+ and Why It Matters

NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) is a coenzyme found in every cell of your body. It plays essential roles in:

Energy production: NAD+ shuttles electrons in the mitochondria (the cell's "power plants") to generate ATP, the primary molecule your cells use for fuel.

DNA repair: When your DNA is damaged by stress, UV light or normal metabolic processes, NAD+ helps activate key repair enzymes.

Cellular communication: NAD+ influences signals that control inflammation, aging pathways and cell survival.

As we age or face chronic stress, NAD+ levels naturally decline. Lower NAD+ can mean less energy, slower recovery from tough days and reduced mental clarity—common complaints among office professionals balancing heavy workloads.

Why Medical Supervision Matters

Self-experimenting with NAD+ boosters can feel tempting, but there are important reasons to involve a doctor:

Complex health backgrounds: Hypertension, thyroid disorders or autoimmune conditions may influence how you respond.

Drug interactions: NAD+ boosters can interact with certain medications (e.g., chemotherapy agents, blood thinners).

Quality control: A medical practice often sources pharmaceutical-grade NAD+ or precursors, reducing the risk of contaminants.

Lab testing: Baseline and follow-up tests (e.g., blood NAD+ levels, kidney and liver function) help optimize dosing and detect issues early.
