What are potential reasons for numbness in both my face and lips?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Facial and lip numbness is often caused by nerve-related conditions such as trigeminal neuropathy, or by underlying issues affecting blood vessels, nerves, or circulation. Common causes include nerve compression, migraines, allergic reactions, vitamin deficiencies (like B12), dental problems, multiple sclerosis, stroke, or side effects from certain medications. Because numbness in the face and lips can range from a minor, temporary sensation to a warning sign of a serious neurological event, identifying the underlying cause quickly is essential for proper treatment.

Since symptoms overlap across many possible conditions, guessing can delay the care you need. Explanation

Numbness in the face and lips can happen for several reasons. One possible cause is trigeminal neuropathy, which affects the nerve responsible for feeling in the face. This condition can lead to numbness, tingling, or pain in facial areas.

Another potential reason is problems with blood vessels, like those seen in certain carotid artery conditions. These can cause unusual sensations, including numbness or a feeling of discomfort in the face.
