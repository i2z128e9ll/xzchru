Finasteride from Keeps

Finasteride is one of the most popular FDA-approved treatments for stopping male pattern baldness. At Keeps, the finasteride-only subscription includes 90 1mg tablets; you’ll take one tablet daily. Studies show that it can stop hair loss and regrow hair for many people with male pattern hair loss when taken accordingly.10 You should not expect to see any regrowth for around four months, but consistently taking it should yield results.

Better known by the brand name Propecia®, finasteride is a type of drug called a 5-alpha reductase inhibitor. 5-alpha reductase is a substance that converts testosterone into dihydrotestosterone (DHT). This conversion process plays a significant role in male pattern hair loss, as excess levels of DHT are a key player in androgenetic alopecia. Finasteride and other drugs of this kind treat hair loss by preventing the conversion of testosterone into DHT in the first place.

This treatment program is the best for men over the age of 20 who have a receding hairline. But finasteride is also effective for male pattern baldness in general. Two-thirds of men with male pattern hair loss at the hairline successfully stop the hair loss using finasteride. Four out of five men with crown/vertex hair loss succeed in eliminating their hair loss with finasteride.2

Side effects and safety considerations

Finasteride requires a prescription because it isn’t suitable or safe for all men. You can get your prescription easily through Keeps’ medical consultation as long as the Keeps doctor concludes that you have no underlying medical conditions that make finasteride unsafe for you. That said, finasteride has a side effect profile that includes potentially significant sexual side effects in 2-3% of patients.11 Those men may experience symptoms of erectile dysfunction, problems with ejaculation, or loss of libido.

It’s important to note that there is controversy around finasteride, with some studies showing more significant risk than others. Many of the studies purporting the highest risk levels — notably including sexual side effects — look at 5mg doses designed to fight benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), as opposed to the much smaller, 1mg dose used for hair loss.12 That alone makes the results hard to compare.

Studies also don’t seem to agree on the long-term safety of the drug. At the 5mg dose, some studies purport symptoms of androgen deficiency over the course of many years.13 But few studies look at the long-term effects of 1mg finasteride as they pertain to healthy hormone levels.

If you notice any such side effects, you can use the Keeps messaging system to reach out to your doctor or schedule a consultation to discuss your options.

How minoxidil works

Minoxidil works differently than 5-alpha reductase inhibitors like finasteride. When used topically for hair loss, only 2% of it absorbs deeper than your scalp. Minoxidil’s original purpose was blood pressure reduction as an oral medication, but early patients reported unwanted hair growth, which led to studies of its topical potential.

Precisely how minoxidil works for hair loss is still not completely clear, but it acts as a vasodilator in the scalp, widening the blood vessels there and promoting an increase in blood flow. The resulting increased delivery of oxygen and nutrients in the blood may be what helps men fight hair loss.3

Additionally, the increased blood flow seems to encourage ailing hair follicles to jump into a new hair growth phase (called the anagen phase). And some studies have found a potential link to hair growth through a change in gene expression caused by minoxidil.

Two-thirds of men can stop their male pattern hair loss using minoxidil. About half report hair regrowth at the top of the head, and 20% of those using minoxidil at the hairline see noticeable regrowth there.

Once you apply minoxidil, you have to leave it in place for four hours. That means you shouldn’t apply it too soon before showering or going to bed, as the latter action can result in a transfer to your pillowcase and then to other parts of your body where you may not want to encourage new hair growth.

Topical finasteride and minoxidil combination

A more recent application for finasteride has been topical, used in the same way that minoxidil has been applied for decades. The idea that led to the research was simple: could topical application have a positive effect on hair loss and a noticeable reduction in sexual side effects enough to supplant oral finasteride? For the most part, the answer is yes. Studies have shown that topical finasteride in a combined solution with minoxidil performs about as well as the oral finasteride and topical minoxidil combination.

The best part of the topical finasteride approach is that it virtually eliminates sexual side effects, with a more than 100-fold decrease in plasma levels of finasteride compared to the oral medication.

This is a prescription-strength antifungal shampoo. It doesn’t stop or treat hair loss directly but instead aims to combat severe dandruff or scalp itchiness that doesn’t respond to over-the-counter alternatives.

The Keeps 2% ketoconazole shampoo subscription includes a bottle of 4 fl oz of the shampoo delivered every three months.14 If you use roughly one teaspoon of it to wash your hair two or three times per week, it should last that long.

Most cases of dandruff result from a sensitivity to a common fungus called Malassezia globosa.15 This fungus is present on almost every human scalp, feeding on the natural oils there. But some people are significantly more sensitive to its presence, which can cause dryness, itchiness, and flaking.

That skin damage harms hair follicles and only gets worse when those follicles get gummed up with excess dead skin cells. By using an antifungal shampoo, you can reduce the presence of Malassezia globosa on the scalp, reducing stress on the follicles you need to protect if you want to fight hair loss.
