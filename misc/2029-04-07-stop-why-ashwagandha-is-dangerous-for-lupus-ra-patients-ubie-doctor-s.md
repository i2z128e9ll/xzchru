Why Your Doctor Cautions Against Ashwagandha in Lupus or RA Patients

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Can ashwagandha worsen autoimmune conditions? Yes. Ashwagandha may trigger flares in lupus and rheumatoid arthritis (RA) by stimulating immune activity and interfering with immunosuppressive medications. This can worsen joint pain, rashes, and other autoimmune symptoms. Additionally, its hormonal effects and common side effects—such as gastrointestinal upset—can further complicate disease management.

Ashwagandha—a popular adaptogenic herb—has gained attention for its stress-relief and immune-modulating properties. While many people turn to ashwagandha to improve overall well-being, those with autoimmune conditions such as lupus (systemic lupus erythematosus, SLE) or rheumatoid arthritis (RA) need to exercise caution. Below, we explain why your doctor may advise against ashwagandha use if you have lupus or RA, using clear, concise language and credible medical insights.

What Is Ashwagandha?

Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) is a traditional herb in Ayurvedic medicine. It's often used for:

Reducing stress and anxiety

Improving energy and stamina

Supporting hormonal balance

Modulating immune function

Its active compounds—called withanolides—are believed to influence cortisol levels and immune cell activity.

Understanding Autoimmune Disease

Autoimmune diseases occur when the body's immune system mistakenly attacks its own tissues. Two common forms are:

Lupus (SLE)– Can affect skin, joints, kidneys, brain, and other organs– Symptoms include joint pain, fatigue, rashes, and organ inflammation

Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA)– Primarily targets joints, causing pain, swelling, and stiffness– Can also involve the lungs, heart, and eyes over time

Both conditions feature an overactive immune response. Treatments often involve suppressing or modulating immune activity to reduce inflammation and prevent organ damage.

How Ashwagandha Affects the Immune System

While ashwagandha is often labeled an "immune booster," it actually acts as an immunomodulator—meaning it can both stimulate and regulate immune activity. Key effects include:

Increasing certain white blood cells (e.g., natural killer cells)

Modulating cytokine production (signaling proteins that drive inflammation)

Potentially enhancing antibody responses

For healthy individuals, modest immune stimulation can be beneficial. In autoimmune disease, however, any increase in immune activity can risk worsening symptoms or triggering flares.

Why Doctors Advise Caution in Lupus and RA

1. Potential to Trigger Flares

Ashwagandha's immune-stimulating properties may intensify the underlying overactivity in lupus or RA, leading to:

Increased joint pain and swelling

More frequent or severe skin rashes in lupus

Heightened fatigue or organ inflammation

2. Interaction with Immunosuppressive Medications

Many lupus and RA patients take medications such as corticosteroids, methotrexate, azathioprine, or biologic agents. Ashwagandha may:

Alter drug metabolism, changing medication levels in the blood

Ashwagandha can influence thyroid function and sex hormones. This is especially important if you're:

Reported Side Effects and Safety Concerns

While generally well tolerated in healthy adults, ashwagandha can cause:

Gastrointestinal upset (nausea, diarrhea)

Drowsiness or sedation (especially in higher doses)

Headaches or dizziness

Rare allergic reactions

For someone with autoimmune disease, these side effects may overlap with existing symptoms, making it harder to pinpoint flare triggers and adjust treatment.

Practical Tips for Patients

If you have lupus or RA and are considering ashwagandha, discuss it with your doctor first. Here are some points to cover:

Current disease activity and recent flare history

All medications and supplements you're taking

Lab values (e.g., white blood cell count, liver and kidney function tests)

Any history of thyroid or hormonal imbalances

Your physician may recommend:

Avoiding ashwagandha entirely

Monitoring more frequently if you decide to try a low dose

Opting for lifestyle approaches to support immunity (stress reduction, balanced diet, sleep hygiene)

When to Seek Medical Advice

Autoimmune diseases can be unpredictable. If you notice any of the following after starting ashwagandha, contact your healthcare provider immediately:

Sudden increase in joint pain or swelling

New rashes, mouth sores, or photosensitivity (common in lupus)

Unusual fatigue, fever, or signs of infection

Abnormal lab results (e.g., elevated inflammatory markers)

Balancing Herbal Support and Autoimmune Care

Herbal and lifestyle interventions can play a supportive role in chronic disease management—but they must be integrated safely:

Prioritize evidence-based therapies prescribed by your rheumatologist

Use stress-management techniques (meditation, yoga, gentle exercise)

Maintain a balanced diet rich in anti-inflammatory foods (omega-3s, colorful fruits and veggies)

Get adequate sleep and avoid overexertion
