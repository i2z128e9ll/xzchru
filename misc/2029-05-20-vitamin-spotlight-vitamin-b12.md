Vitamin B12 Deficiency: More Common Than You Might Think

Vitamin B12 is present in many types of food that are a common part of many peoples’ diets. As a result, most people can get an adequate intake of this vitamin from their diet alone. However, it’s estimated that a Vitamin B12 deficiency impacts up to 15% of the population.

Absorption is the primary reason that people develop a Vitamin B12 deficiency, whether due to a medical condition or medications that inhibit your body’s natural absorption mechanism. Vegetarians, vegans, and elderly people are at the highest risk of developing a deficiency in this vitamin. In addition, people who have had gastrointestinal surgery may have a reduced ability to absorb this vitamin naturally from food.

A vitamin B12 deficiency is characterized by several possible symptoms. You may experience one or several of the symptoms listed below:

Megaloblastic anemia

Fatigue

Weakness

Constipation

Loss of appetite

Weight loss

Neurological changes (eg.numbness and tingling in the hands and feet)

Difficulty maintaining balance

Vitamin B12 Oral Supplements, IV Infusions, or Shots?

There are three ways to supplement your Vitamin B12 intake: with an oral supplement, an IV infusion, or a B12 shot. Individuals with a Vitamin B12 deficiency can benefit from all of these avenues; however, certain people may have difficulties absorbing vitamins orally due to medication or a preexisting health condition. In these cases, shots or infusions may be a more effective option, as B12 shots and infusions are delivered directly into the bloodstream for a 100% absorption rate.

Whether you choose an oral supplement, an IV infusion, or a B12 shot, the goal of getting a Vitamin B12 supplement is the same: to ensure that you are getting enough of this vitamin to stay in top health. Not sure whether an oral supplement, IV infusion, or shot is right for you? Let’s take a closer look at each option.

Oral Supplements

Grabbing a Vitamin B12 oral supplement is as easy as placing an online order or stopping by your local drugstore, a convenient option for most people since an oral supplement is as easy as taking a vitamin daily.

Oral Vitamin B12 supplements are absorbed by your digestive system, which impacts how much your body can actually use. Oral supplement absorption rates vary from person to person due to factors such as circulatory systems, gastric emptying time, and individual metabolic function. This means that your body may only use a portion of an oral supplement and excrete the remaining amount.

One big advantage of oral supplements is that they are very safe and you are not at risk of developing an infection as you may with an IV infusion or shot. As a result, oral supplements may be ideal for certain individuals, especially those who have an immune system disorder.

Pros: Readily available over-the-counter at your local pharmacy; an inexpensive option; very safe and generally well-tolerated.

Cons: Not absorbed as well as other options and may take some trial and error to determine the right dose for you to correct a deficiency.
