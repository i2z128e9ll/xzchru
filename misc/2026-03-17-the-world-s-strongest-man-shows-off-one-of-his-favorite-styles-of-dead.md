# The World's Strongest Man Shows Off One of His Favorite Styles of Deadlift

Strongman Martins Licis is still on the road to recovery from being hit by a car back in the summer. While he's skipping out on this year's World's Strongest Man competition, he's still training and healing his body ahead of his next goal, winning the Arnold Strongman Classic in March 2021. To do so, he's working on his eccentric deadlift.

"It's been a long time. I think over a year," says Licis. "Last year I was in repair mode, even leading up to World's Strongest Man. My right hip was hurting so much that I couldn't do any crazy deadlifting."

He's looking to try these eccentric moves to "keep his strength up without destroying my hip too much."

Before his gets to the deadlifts, he does some pullups to "activate the lats and get the grip strong."

After his set of 10, he hops on a scale to quickly check his weight.

"Ooo, a little on the heavy side... 338 pounds," says Licis.

He knocks out two more sets of pullups, then gets to his deadlifts. He uses a metronome to makes sure he is keeping a steady rhythm for the eccentric, or lowering, portion of the movement. After warming up with a few reps, he loads the bar up with 517 pounds.

After one rep, he takes some weight off to "contrast that with some speed work." The bar is at 385 pounds, and he pulls through three reps. Then, he loads the bar back up to 517 pounds for four more sets. For the eccentric portion of the movements, he lowers the bar down slowly for 15 seconds.

He continues to rotate between lighter, faster reps and heavier, slower reps. He then shares that his workout the previous Sunday was ruined.

"I lost all motivation because there were too many people talking in the gym. Which normally nothing stops me from getting in a good workout, but I've been using bullshit excuses to stop lifting as hard as I can," says Licis.

He says his training partner Romark sent him a text telling him that "if you're gonna f*cking do it, do it all the way," and it got his motivation back for his workout as he preps for the Arnold competition.

But another issue is a tear in his hip that he's been dealing with "for two years."

"It's been getting progressively worse. All this time I thought it was my hamstring, turns out it was a little tear in the posterior side of my labral. I've taken the last half year to figure out what helps it, Dr. Aaron Horschig from Squat University has helped tremendously to build strength and stability in my hip," says Licis. "Basically I've stopped doing any motions I feel that pain in. And I've been struggling to find a way to deadlift heavy. So I'm very happy with how these deadlifts felt today."

Next he moves on to single arm hangs to develop his core and grip strength. He holds each one for 10 seconds. Once he's done a few sets on each arm, he moves on to single arm dumbbell rows, followed by bent over rows using 75 pound dumbbells.
