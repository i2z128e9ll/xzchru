First Impressions of the Expedition Parka

When I first received the jacket, I was a little nervous: this thing felt dense. I felt like I had just hit a shoulder workout on the trip to my car. I shouldn't have been too surprised, though. The jacket was originally developed in the ‘80s for scientists studying in Antarctica. In addition to this fun factoid, I found another impressive stat: the Expedition is designed for temperatures of -22 degrees and below. Suffice to say, I won't be going on any Arctic expeditions anytime soon, but I run pretty cold, and at times, New York in January feels like Antarctica.

This may sound a little unhinged, and definitely has nothing to do with the jacket's performance itself, but it might be the best-smelling jacket out of the box I've ever encountered. Not sure what they're using in the Canada Goose store, but I'd love a whole bottle of it. Or seven.

Fit and Comfort

Canada Goose's proprietary Arctic Tech material (an 83 percent polyester and 17 percent cotton blend) makes up the outer shell, finished with a water-repellent treatment. Inside, 625 fill down makes up 80 percent of the insulation, rounded out with feathers. The jacket runs big, and the site recommends sizing down if you don't plan on layering. There's no real need to layer with this behemoth, but I prefer my jackets to run a little big anyway. I went true to size, but admittedly, I'm between sizes, so I always err on the side of caution.

I definitely made the right choice. The jacket, while oversized, fit perfectly. And while I was initially concerned about its weight, it was surprisingly light when I had it on. Most parkas I’ve tested feel heavy on my shoulders after a few hours, but this wasn't the case with the Expedition. I guess this speaks to its original design—its use case required all-day comfort and warmth for expedition scientists.

The jacket features an interior drawcord at the waist for a customized, tapered fit that really traps heat, so I’m never shivering. As a bonus, it gives the jacket a certain stylish elegance—a rare feat for a parka. Another cool feature: recessed rib knit cuffs. The stretchy cuffs almost feel vacuum-sealed to your wrists, without sacrificing comfort. This also adds to the Expedition's already impressive insulation, which allowed me to wear the jacket open on a warmer day.

The two-way zipper is a prime example of how well-made this piece of outerwear is. It's thicker than any I've seen on a jacket and feels incredibly durable. It almost looks like it can take your finger off if you're not careful (it won't, but you get the idea). A velcro placket with velcro closures can seal the jacket even further, so no cold drafts get in there.

Storage is certainly not a concern with the Expedition, either. The jacket comes with four double-entry Velcro pockets with a zippered side access, which is enough for your everyday carry. There's a utility pocket on the left sleeve and a more robust Velcro-closure pocket on the right. Inside, a zippered security pocket and two mesh drop-in pockets round out the storage options. Plus, there’s a chin guard, an adjustable down hood, and an elasticized snow skirt to keep you as warm as humanely possible.

Final Thoughts: Is it Worth It?

My answer is, unequivocally, yes. I go back to my first point: you're investing here. The $1,600 price tag may understandably scare some people off, but considering a good parka will typically run north of $400—and that you'll likely buy several over your lifetime—you'll end up saving money in the long run, even before factoring in inflation. The Expedition genuinely feels like it will last several lifetimes, and you'd have to essentially go toe-to-toe with a gorilla to do serious damage to it. Plus, it comes with a lifetime warranty when purchased from an authorized Canada Goose retailer. I'd certainly consider it a low-risk investment.
