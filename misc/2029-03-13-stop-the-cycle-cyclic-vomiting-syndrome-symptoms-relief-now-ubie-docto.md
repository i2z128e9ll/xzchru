Internal Medicine

Cyclic vomiting syndrome (CVS) is a chronic disorder marked by sudden, repeated episodes of severe vomiting, nausea, and stomach pain, followed by symptom-free periods. Treatment focuses on three goals: rehydrating the body with IV or oral fluids, easing nausea with anti-emetic medications, and preventing future episodes with preventive drugs such as antimigraine or antidepressant medications.

Because CVS symptoms overlap with many other conditions—like migraines, gastroparesis, or food intolerances—getting an accurate read on what's happening in your body is the critical first step. With cyclic vomiting syndrome, you experience episodes that start suddenly with lots of vomiting, stomach pain, and feeling very sick. These episodes can last for hours or even days, and there are breaks when you feel almost normal. Along with vomiting, some people may feel very tired, have a headache, or be sensitive to light.

During an episode, doctors work to make you feel better by giving you fluids so you do not get dehydrated. They also use medicines that help stop nausea and control pain. Sometimes, if the vomiting keeps coming back, doctors may prescribe preventive medicines like those used for migraines or even anti-seizure drugs.

It is also important to look for things that set off the episodes, such as stress or certain foods, or even certain substances that can induce this, such as cannabis, and try to avoid them.
