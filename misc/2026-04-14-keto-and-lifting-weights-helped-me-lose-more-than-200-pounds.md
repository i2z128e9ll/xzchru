# Keto and Lifting Weights Helped Me Lose More Than 200 Pounds

Adam Harris lives in Spencer, IA, and works in IT management. After serious back surgery, he re-evaluated his relationship with his body and decided he needed to get healthy. In his own words, here is how he did it.

Since childhood I’ve been a picky eater. Anything considered a vegetable had no place on my plate. I’d load up on meat and carbs in huge portions, and for a long time that worked. In my early twenties I was a healthy weight, though it started to creep up in my mid-twenties and early thirties. I started getting back pain, which I let go untreated, killing my activity levels. My weight gain really picked up, and by 35 I was at 430 pounds.

I felt like I was on death's door. I was on oxygen, could hardly walk with a walker, and couldn’t manage basic functions like tying my shoes while on my feet. My life was really miserable, but somehow that still wasn’t enough to make me change.

I ended up in the hospital for back surgery, which then led to complications. My legs didn’t work the way they should have coming out of surgery, and four days later I was slated to go to a care center for recovery.

I did a lot of soul-searching that night, first blaming the world for my situation, then getting angry at myself as the true culprit. I really thought this was the end. A thought that came up sticks with me to this day: If this was all a game, and I had one life, did I play it right? Framing the question that way immediately sparked my desire for change.

I worked through the night with a nurse, until I could walk across the room and back. I didn’t have to go to recovery, and resolved to get healthy.

Diet was my first major change. I had a few false starts, until I saw a Reddit thread about keto for picky eaters. I gave it a shot. Soon I was learning about macros and tracking them religiously. I started thinking of food as a means of survival rather than a source of entertainment. I was eating 2200 calories a day, and within a year I’d lost 100 pounds through diet changes alone.

At that point I had a spinal stimulator installed to help with the residual pain. After recovering I started at the gym, first swimming laps in the morning, then eventually finding my way to the guided weight machines. I lost another 115 pounds over the next year through diet and exercise. At one point I’d lost a total of 225 pounds.

I'll never forget the first time I realized tying my shoes was not a chore, or the first time I looked down and saw my feet again. I still have back and leg issues, but I get around much better, and without a walker. My family has been really supportive, especially my sister. And two years ago I met the woman of my dreams.
