What Is Sciatica? Causes, Symptoms & Treatment Options

Sciatica is nerve pain caused by irritation or compression of the sciatic nerve, often due to a herniated disc, spinal stenosis, bone spurs, or muscle spasms. Common symptoms include sharp, burning, or electric-like pain, numbness, or tingling that radiates from the lower back down one leg.

Key facts about sciatica:

Causes: Herniated discs, spinal stenosis, bone spurs, piriformis muscle spasms

Symptoms: Radiating leg pain, numbness, tingling, muscle weakness

Diagnosis: Evaluation of posture, gait, range of motion, and muscle strength

Self-care: Activity modification, heat/cold therapy, stretching, ergonomic changes, NSAIDs

Advanced care: Physical therapy, pain management, or surgical consultation for persistent or severe cases

What Is Sciatica?

Sciatica isn't a condition on its own but rather a set of symptoms caused by compression or irritation of the sciatic nerve roots (L4–S3). Commonly, the nerve is pinched by:

A herniated (slipped) disc

Muscle spasms in the buttock (piriformis syndrome)

When the nerve is irritated, you may feel pain, numbness or tingling that typically affects only one side of your body.

Common Causes of Leg-Radiating Back Pain

Understanding the root cause helps tailor your treatment. Major contributors include:

Disc Herniation: A bulging disc presses on a nerve root, often triggering sharp pain down the leg.

Degenerative Disc Disease: Age-related wear on spinal discs can lead to nerve compression.

Spinal Stenosis: Narrowing of the spaces within your spine puts pressure on nerves.

Piriformis Syndrome: Tight or spasming hip muscles compress the sciatic nerve.

Spondylolisthesis: A vertebra slips forward over the one below it, narrowing the nerve exit.

Self-Care and Management Strategies

For most people with sciatica, conservative measures provide relief:

Avoid prolonged sitting; take short walking breaks every 30 minutes

Apply ice packs for 10–15 minutes to reduce inflammation

Switch to heat packs after two days to relax muscles

Hamstring stretches (e.g., lying hamstring stretch with a strap)

Pelvic tilts and knee-to-chest stretches

Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) as directed

Supportive chair with lumbar support

Footrest or cushion to tilt your pelvis forward when sitting

Consistency is key: perform stretches twice daily and gradually increase walking time.
