Activated charcoal can cause diarrhea, particularly when it's formulated with sorbitol, a sugar alcohol used to speed elimination. Even without sorbitol, activated charcoal may lead to loose stools, stomach upset, or changes in bowel habits in some people. Other common side effects include constipation, black stools, and nausea.

Because diarrhea can stem from many causes—medications, infections, diet, or underlying conditions—it's important to identify what's actually driving your symptoms rather than guessing. Explanation

Activated charcoal is sometimes used to treat poisonings, but it can have side effects. One side effect is diarrhea, which is more likely to happen when activated charcoal is mixed with sorbitol, a sweetener that can cause bowel movements.

Key Points:

Activated Charcoal: Used for treating certain types of poisonings.

Diarrhea: Can occur, especially if mixed with sorbitol.

Sorbitol: A sweetener that can cause loose stools.

In a study involving healthy volunteers, the combination of activated charcoal and sorbitol was shown to cause diarrhea. This is important to know because diarrhea can lead to dehydration if it is severe or lasts a long time. If you are experiencing persistent diarrhea or other concerning symptoms after taking activated charcoal, consider using a free symptom checker to help assess your condition, and be sure to drink plenty of fluids to stay hydrated.
