# Is It RMD or Seizures? Don’t Risk a Dangerous Misdiagnosis | Ubie Doctor's Note

Published on: 7/21/2026

Can Sleep-Related Rhythmic Movement Disorder (RMD) be mistaken for seizures or epilepsy?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Sleep-Related Rhythmic Movement Disorder (RMD) is often confused with seizures or epilepsy because both conditions involve unusual, repetitive movements during sleep. However, they differ significantly in cause, treatment, and long-term impact.

RMD typically involves rhythmic, repetitive motions—such as head banging, body rocking, or leg rolling—that occur as a person is falling asleep or during light sleep. These movements are usually voluntary in origin and self-soothing. Seizures, in contrast, are caused by abnormal electrical activity in the brain and can happen at any stage of sleep, often accompanied by stiffening, jerking, confusion, or loss of awareness.

Because the symptoms can overlap, accurate diagnosis is essential. If you or a loved one experiences unusual movements during sleep, a free, instant, online symptom check can help clarify possible causes and guide your next steps—whether that means monitoring at home, seeking a sleep specialist, or ruling out a neurological condition. Understanding your symptoms early leads to faster answers and better rest.
