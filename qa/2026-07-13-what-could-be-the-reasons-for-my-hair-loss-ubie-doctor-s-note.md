What could be the reasons for my hair loss?

Reviewed by Suo Lee, MD

Internal Medicine

Hair loss can happen due to genetics, stress, hormonal changes, or even certain medications.

Explanation

Hair loss, also known as alopecia, can be caused by various factors. Genetic factors are a common cause, where hair loss patterns run in families. This type is often called androgenetic alopecia. Stress and hormonal changes, such as those occurring during pregnancy or menopause, can also lead to hair loss. Additionally, certain medications may cause hair to fall out. For example, some drugs used for treating cancer, depression, or heart problems can impact hair growth.

Here are some common reasons for hair loss:

Genetic Factors: Family history can influence hair loss patterns.

Hormonal Changes: Events like pregnancy, childbirth, or menopause can affect hair.

Medical Conditions: Illnesses such as thyroid problems or autoimmune diseases can cause hair loss.

Medications: Some drugs, like those for cancer treatment, may lead to hair loss.

Stress and Lifestyle: High stress levels and poor nutrition may contribute to hair loss.

Understanding the cause of hair loss can help in finding the right treatment or management strategy.
