What diseases are commonly associated with drooping eyelids?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Drooping eyelids, medically known as ptosis, occur when the upper eyelid falls lower than normal over the eye. Ptosis can affect one or both eyes and may develop suddenly or gradually over time.

Common causes of drooping eyelids include:

Nerve problems – Damage to the nerves controlling eyelid muscles, often linked to conditions like Horner syndrome or third cranial nerve palsy.

Muscle disorders – Weakness or deterioration of the levator muscle, which lifts the eyelid.

Myasthenia gravis – An autoimmune disease that causes muscle weakness, including in the eyelids.

Aging – Natural stretching of tendons and muscles over time.

Injury or trauma – Damage to the eye area or surrounding nerves.
