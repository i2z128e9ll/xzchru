Is 110 Too High for a Fasting Blood Sugar? (Prediabetes Check)

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

A fasting blood sugar of 110 mg/dL falls within the prediabetes range (100–125 mg/dL), meaning your body isn't processing glucose efficiently. While this reading signals an increased risk for type 2 diabetes and heart disease, prediabetes is often reversible through targeted lifestyle changes like improved diet, regular exercise, and weight management.

Confirmation typically requires repeat fasting tests, an A1C measurement, or an oral glucose tolerance test. Because elevated blood sugar can accompany other subtle symptoms—fatigue, increased thirst, blurred vision, or frequent urination—it's important to look at the full picture.

Explanation

A fasting blood sugar (FBS) test measures the level of glucose in your blood after you've gone without food or drink (except water) for at least eight hours. When you see a result of "glucose 110 fasting," you may wonder whether that's within a healthy range or a sign of prediabetes. Understanding what this number means—and what to do next—can help you take control of your health without unnecessary worry.

Understanding Fasting Blood Sugar Levels

Health experts agree on the following categories for fasting blood glucose (measured in mg/dL):

Normal: Less than 100 mg/dL

Prediabetes: 100–125 mg/dL

Diabetes: 126 mg/dL or higher (on two separate tests)

A reading of 110 mg/dL falls squarely into the prediabetes range. Prediabetes isn't an illness you can "catch" like a cold, but it does mean your body isn't processing glucose as efficiently as it should.

What Does Prediabetes Mean?

Prediabetes indicates higher-than-normal blood sugar, but not high enough for a diabetes diagnosis. Key points include:

Impaired Insulin Response: Your body either doesn't make enough insulin or cells aren't responding to insulin properly.

Risk Factor: It raises your risk of developing type 2 diabetes, heart disease, and stroke.

Reversible: In many cases, lifestyle changes can return blood sugar to a normal range.

Knowing your status gives you the chance to act early and prevent progression.

Who's at Risk?

Certain factors make prediabetes more likely:

Family history of type 2 diabetes

Overweight or obesity (especially around the waist)

Why 110 Isn't Doom and Gloom

Seeing "prediabetes" can feel alarming, but it's also a window of opportunity:

Early Warning: You're catching metabolic changes before full-blown diabetes sets in.

Modifiable with Lifestyle: Diet, exercise, and weight management can lower fasting glucose.

Improves Overall Health: Steps taken to manage prediabetes often improve heart and kidney health, too.

The goal is to prevent progression and protect your long-term wellness.

Next Steps After a 110 mg/dL Reading

Repeat the Test

Blood sugar can fluctuate. A second fasting test confirms whether 110 mg/dL is consistent.

Your doctor may also order an HbA₁c test to measure average blood sugar over 2–3 months.

Lifestyle Changes That Can Help

Small, sustainable changes often yield the best results:

Nutrition

Focus on whole foods: vegetables, fruits, whole grains, lean proteins, and healthy fats

Limit refined carbs and added sugars

Control portion sizes and avoid "mindless" snacking

Physical Activity

Aim for 150 minutes of moderate exercise per week (e.g., brisk walking, cycling)

Include strength training at least twice weekly

Break up long periods of sitting with short walks or stretches

Weight Management

Losing even 5–7% of body weight can significantly reduce blood sugar

Work with a dietitian or certified coach for personalized support

Stress Management

Practice relaxation techniques: deep breathing, meditation, yoga

Prioritize sleep (7–9 hours per night)

When to Seek Professional Advice

A single "glucose 110 fasting" result shouldn't cause panic, but it does mean you should stay proactive. Consider these warning signs that warrant prompt medical attention:

Excessive thirst or urination

Unexplained fatigue or weight loss

Blurred vision or slow-healing sores

Recurrent infections

If you experience any of these symptoms—or if your blood sugar readings climb closer to the diabetes range—please speak to a doctor.

Talking to Your Healthcare Provider

When you meet your doctor, be prepared to discuss:

Your fasting glucose results and any other recent tests (HbA₁c, cholesterol, blood pressure)

Family history of diabetes or heart disease

Diet, exercise habits, sleep patterns, and stress levels

Current medications and supplements

Monitoring Your Progress

Consistency is key. Establish a routine:

Check fasting blood sugar weekly or monthly as advised

Keep a log of readings alongside notes on diet, activity, and sleep

Adjust your plan based on trends, not single readings

Many people use smartphone apps or a simple paper journal to stay accountable. Sharing this log with your doctor can help tailor recommendations.

Long-Term Outlook

With proactive steps, most individuals with a glucose 110 fasting result can lower their risk of developing type 2 diabetes. Research shows that lifestyle interventions can reduce progression to diabetes by up to 58%. Achieving and maintaining a healthy blood sugar level also:

Improves energy and mood

Supports cardiovascular health

Protects kidney and nerve function

Remember, early action is protective, not punitive.

Key Takeaways

A fasting blood sugar of 110 mg/dL is classified as prediabetes.

Prediabetes doesn't guarantee you'll develop diabetes; it's a call to action.

Repeat testing, an HbA₁c, or an OGTT can confirm your status.

Healthy eating, regular exercise, weight management, and stress reduction are central to improvement.

Monitor symptoms and seek immediate care if you experience alarming signs.

Speak to your doctor about any serious concerns.

If you have questions about your results or experience worrying symptoms, don't hesitate to speak to a healthcare professional. Early guidance can make all the difference in protecting your long-term health.
