Can sleep apnea cause morning headaches?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

[This Urgent](https://aegispeptide.snaketongueofficial.com/blog/7065997291.html)

Internal Medicine

Sleep apnea can cause morning headaches. During sleep, repeated breathing interruptions lower oxygen levels and raise carbon dioxide levels in the blood, which dilates blood vessels in the brain and triggers head pain upon waking. These headaches typically occur on both sides of the head, last less than four hours, and improve throughout the day.

If you're waking up with headaches, it's worth investigating the root cause. Untreated sleep apnea is linked to serious health risks, including heart disease and stroke, so early identification matters. Explanation

Morning headaches are a common symptom for people with sleep apnea. Sleep apnea is a condition where breathing stops and starts during sleep, which can lead to lower oxygen levels and higher carbon dioxide levels in the blood. These changes can cause the blood vessels in the head to widen, leading to headaches when you wake up.

Here are some key points:

Sleep Apnea Effects: Sleep apnea disrupts normal breathing patterns, which can affect how much oxygen and carbon dioxide are in your blood.
